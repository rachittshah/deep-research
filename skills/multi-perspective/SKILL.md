# Multi-Perspective Research Framework

Generate distinct researcher personas to prevent single-perspective tunnel vision. Based on Stanford STORM's multi-perspective approach.

## Perspective Generation

For any research topic, generate 3-5 distinct researcher personas. Each persona must have genuinely different viewpoints — not just different sub-topics.

### Persona Template

```
Persona: [Name / Role]
Expertise: [Domain and specialization]
Viewpoint: [Core perspective, assumptions, potential biases]
Question Style: [How they approach inquiry]
Priorities: [What they emphasize and what they might downplay]
Preferred Sources: [What evidence they trust most]
```

### Example Personas for a Technology Topic

| Persona | Viewpoint | Priorities |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Academic Researcher | Rigorous methodology, peer-reviewed evidence | Reproducibility, statistical significance, literature context |
| Industry Practitioner | Real-world applicability, scale constraints | Performance benchmarks, cost, integration complexity |
| Skeptic / Devil's Advocate | Challenge assumptions, seek disconfirmation | Edge cases, failure modes, hidden costs, overhyped claims |
| End-User Advocate | Accessibility, usability, actual impact | User experience, learning curve, real-world adoption |
| Policy / Ethics Analyst | Societal impact, regulatory landscape | Fairness, privacy, long-term consequences, compliance |

## Common Perspective Archetypes by Domain

### Technology / Engineering
- Academic Researcher, Industry Practitioner, Skeptic, End-User Advocate, Security Analyst

### Healthcare / Science
- Clinical Researcher, Practicing Clinician, Patient Advocate, Regulatory Expert, Health Economist

### Business / Strategy
- Market Analyst, Operations Leader, Customer Advocate, Financial Analyst, Competitor Strategist

### Policy / Social Issues
- Policy Researcher, Affected Community Member, Economic Analyst, Legal Expert, Historical Contextualist

### Finance / Economics
- Quantitative Analyst, Institutional Investor, Retail Investor Advocate, Regulatory Perspective, Macroeconomist

## Perspective Assignment

Map each research angle to one or more personas:

1. **Identify research angles** from the research plan
2. **Assign primary perspective** — which persona owns this angle?
3. **Assign challenge perspective** — which persona would push back on findings?
4. **Ensure coverage** — every angle has at least one primary and one challenger

### Assignment Table Format

| Research Angle | Primary Perspective | Challenge Perspective |
|----------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| [angle 1] | [persona] | [persona] |
| [angle 2] | [persona] | [persona] |

## How Perspectives Influence Research

### Search Queries
Each persona generates different search queries for the same topic:
- **Academic**: "[topic] systematic review", "[topic] meta-analysis", "[topic] peer-reviewed"
- **Practitioner**: "[topic] implementation guide", "[topic] benchmarks", "[topic] case study"
- **Skeptic**: "[topic] criticism", "[topic] limitations", "[topic] failed", "[topic] overhyped"
- **End-User**: "[topic] tutorial", "[topic] comparison", "[topic] real-world experience"
- **Policy**: "[topic] regulation", "[topic] ethics", "[topic] societal impact"

### Source Preferences
- **Academic**: Peer-reviewed journals, conference proceedings, preprints from reputable groups
- **Practitioner**: Technical documentation, engineering blogs from major companies, benchmarks
- **Skeptic**: Critical reviews, post-mortems, independent audits
- **End-User**: Community forums, user reviews, hands-on comparison articles
- **Policy**: Government reports, NGO publications, legal analyses

### Evaluation Criteria
Each persona weights evidence differently:
- **Academic**: Methodology rigor > sample size > peer review status
- **Practitioner**: Scale evidence > production readiness > cost analysis
- **Skeptic**: Replication status > conflict of interest > alternative explanations
- **End-User**: Ease of use > accessibility > community support
- **Policy**: Population impact > equity considerations > precedent

## Synthesis: Balancing Perspectives

When combining findings across perspectives:

1. **Equal initial weight** — no perspective is privileged by default
2. **Evidence-based reweighting** — perspectives backed by stronger evidence get more weight
3. **Explicit disagreements** — when perspectives conflict, present both views with evidence
4. **Blind spots** — note what each perspective might miss; the synthesis should cover gaps
5. **Confidence annotation** — mark claims where all perspectives agree (high confidence) vs. where they diverge (note the divergence)

### Synthesis Checklist
- [ ] Every perspective's key findings are represented
- [ ] Disagreements between perspectives are explicitly noted
- [ ] No single perspective dominates without evidence justification
- [ ] Blind spots from each perspective are addressed by others
- [ ] Final conclusions acknowledge remaining uncertainty
